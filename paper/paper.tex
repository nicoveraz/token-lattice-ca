\title{A Validated Black-Box Instrument for Language-Model Dynamics:\\
Reproducing Known Metrics with a Token-Lattice Cellular Automaton}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We turn a language model into a \emph{cellular automaton over token space} --- a ring of token
cells resampled in place by the model's own windowed conditional $p_r(x_i\mid x_{i\pm r})$, probed
by common-random-number damage spreading --- and develop it into a \emph{validated} measurement
instrument, on the principle of \textbf{validation by reproduction}. On the Domany--Kinzel
automaton, where the damage field is provably the automaton itself, an independent prediction
matches ours \textbf{bit-exactly} --- zero mismatching cells, with a nonzero off-line control ---
a calibration with no error bar attached, which a plausible-looking but wrong implementation
cannot pass. The instrument also separates
elementary CA rules by whether damage survives ($d{=}3.0$) and recovers known Markov matrices.
Across Pythia-410m checkpoints it detects a \textbf{developmental transition}: the token-space
Lyapunov exponent goes from seeds disagreeing about its \emph{sign} to \textbf{every one of 48
post-transition runs being positive}, at two lattice sizes, all four members of a pre-registered
family surviving Benjamini--Hochberg correction. We then \emph{delimit} it, the result we consider
most useful: real autoregressive generation absorbs \emph{no} injected token error
($P_{\text{persist}}{=}1.000$ on three models), because free generation never resamples a token.
These quantities characterise an iterated-resampling construction, not deployed decoding --- a
boundary we draw rather than leave to the reader.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Language models are evaluated almost entirely as functions: given a context, how good is the
next-token distribution? But a masked or autoregressive conditional also \emph{defines a dynamics}
--- iterate it in place over a lattice of tokens and one obtains a stochastic cellular automaton
whose rule is the model. The phenomena a CA vocabulary names --- damage spreading
\citep{bagnoli1992damage}, light cones \citep{lieb1972finite}, Lyapunov exponents, attractor
censuses \citep{hanson1997computational} --- are decades old. What has been missing is not a
phenomenon but a \emph{measurement instrument whose readings can be trusted}.

Our organizing principle is therefore \textbf{validation by reproduction}
(\S\ref{sec:validation}): before turning the apparatus on a language model we require it to
recover values that are independently known. Damage-spreading implementations are conventionally
validated against fitted critical exponents, which a wrong implementation can match for the wrong
reasons; our strongest rung is instead \emph{exact}, and a bit-exact identity cannot be passed by
a plausible-looking estimator. Only then do we report what the instrument measures on trained LMs
(\S\ref{sec:dev}) and, equally, where its readings provably stop applying
(\S\ref{sec:realgen}, \S\ref{sec:crosslevel}).

\textbf{Rule} (Fig.~\ref{fig:instrument}). A window of $w{=}2r{+}1$ tokens
with the centre masked is fed to a masked LM; the tempered centre distribution is the kernel
$p_r(x_i\mid x_{i\pm r})$. For an autoregressive model the window is the $r$ cells to the left.
\textbf{Confounds.} A degenerate low-entropy lattice
snaps a perturbation back trivially, so damage $D$ is normalised by the model's own diversity
floor \dz{} (twins under \emph{independent} noise, no flip), giving $\dn{=}D/\dz$. The floor's coupling cost is bounded (App.~\ref{app:robust}). A free control disciplines our language: the
temperature ``phase transition'' is a finite-size \emph{crossover}
($\chi_{\mathrm{peak}}\propto1/N$).
\section{Validation: reproducing known metrics}\label{sec:validation}
We check the apparatus against systems where the answer is known, ordered by how much of the
instrument's regime each rung shares (Fig.~\ref{fig:ladder}, App.~\ref{app:robust}). \textbf{(1--2)} are smooth-limit
unit tests, labelled as such: the logistic map's analytic exponent is recovered to machine
precision and a coupled-map lattice matches an exact Benettin reference to $1.1\times10^{-3}$;
neither is evidence about damage spreading --- the twin is re-anchored each step, and
\textbf{a token flip is $O(1)$}.

\textbf{(3) Elementary CA: a coarse split.} The identical protocol on 19 rules of \emph{known}
class (12 seeds, rule as the unit) separates damage that \emph{survives} from damage that
\emph{dies}. The discriminating quantity is the \textbf{ignition probability} --- also the
directed-percolation order parameter \citep{grassberger1995} --- not $\lambda$, which averages a
bimodal mixture. Ordered rules sit at $0.046$ (CI $[0.000,0.102]$) against $0.668$ (edge) and
$0.682$ (chaotic): \textbf{ordered vs.\ rest $p{=}0.0$, $d{=}3.03$}. The three-class ordering does
\emph{not} survive ($p{=}0.470$), so we claim only the coarse split; the demoted group-mean
$\lambda$ ordering, and why it was never a measurement, are in App.~\ref{app:robust}.

\textbf{(4) Domany--Kinzel: an \emph{exact} check.} DK \citep{domany1984} is the only rung that
is stochastic \emph{and} discrete --- the instrument's own regime --- and on its $p_2{=}0$ line
the damage field between two commonly-driven replicas is provably \emph{itself} a DK automaton at
the same $p_1$ \citep{kohring1992}. We therefore predict the damage trajectory with an independent
simulator and demand \emph{bit-identity}: across $p_1\in\{0.2,\dots,1.0\}$ on a ring of $4096$
over $1500$ steps, \textbf{zero mismatching cells}, against $16$ in an off-line control at
$(0.6,0.5)$ where the mapping must break. It runs through the same loop as every language-model
number below, so indexing, uniform consumption, sampling and schedule are verified with no error
bar attached; Rule 90 rides inside the same run as a second closed form ($0.03125$ live cells at
$t{=}1500$, as obtained). Critical points are a weaker $\sim\!1\%$ check: $p_c{=}0.7065$ on the
site-DP line (published $0.705489(4)$) and $0.8092$ on $p_2{=}0$, the damage field independently
at $0.8089$. The published $p_2{=}0$ value is disputed ($0.801(2)$ \citep{zebende1994} vs.\
$0.8087(5)$ \citep{hinrichsen1997}), so we report both and claim neither.

\textbf{(5) Synthetic Markov sources.} The attractor census recovers each model's own sparse
transition matrix (row total-variation $0.22$) and not the others (cross $0.95$; baseline
$0.91$).


\section{A developmental transition in token-space criticality}\label{sec:dev}
Pointed at a training run, the calibrated instrument finds a developmental transition:
token-space dynamics cross from sub- to super-critical early in training and do not return.
Across Pythia-410m checkpoints (\texttt{step256}--\texttt{step143000}, 8 seeds, $N{=}48$ and
$96$; Fig.~\ref{fig:dev}) the crossing lies between steps $256$ and $512$ at both lattice sizes,
$\lca$ staying positive thereafter.

\textbf{The effect occupies a temperature window, and the window has a mechanism.} Repeating the
two ends of the curve at $T\in\{0.3,0.5,0.9,1.1\}$ (8 seeds each, BH-FDR over the four) recovers
the transition at $T{=}0.5$ ($p_{\mathrm{BH}}{=}6{\times}10^{-4}$) alongside the $T{=}0.7$ of the
main grid, and at none of $0.3$, $0.9$, $1.1$ ($p_{\mathrm{BH}}{=}0.59$, $0.72$, $0.59$). The
ignition fraction makes that a floor and a ceiling rather than a failure: below the window damage
barely propagates at either end and $\lca$ hugs zero, whereas above it the lattice is
\emph{already} super-critical before the training being measured, leaving nothing to move; inside
the window it swings $0.23\to0.80$ at $T{=}0.5$ (floor and ceiling values in
App.~\ref{app:robust}). Independent work puts Pythia \emph{generation}'s critical
temperature at $T_c{\approx}1$ \citep{ar_tempcrit}; a different sampler, so a consistent reference
point rather than the same measurement --- but a ceiling between $0.7$ and $0.9$ is where it
predicts one.

The test was pre-registered (post- vs pre-transition, run-level Mann--Whitney, family
$=\{\lca,\dn\}\times\{N{=}48,96\}$, BH-FDR) with a demotion rule: failure at either size demotes
the headline. \textbf{All four survive} ($p_{\mathrm{BH}}\le3{\times}10^{-5}$; Table~\ref{tab:dev}).
The pre set is $256/512$ and the plateau $2000/8000/143000$; one $N{=}96$ run is excluded because
its damage never ignited, so $\lca$ is undefined for it.

\textbf{The claim is better stated without any pre/post split}, since every effect size depends
on where that line is drawn. Before the transition the seeds do not agree on the \emph{sign} of
$\lca$ (6/16 negative at $N{=}48$, 7/15 at $N{=}96$, spanning $-0.216$ to $+0.320$); after it,
\textbf{not one of 48 plateau runs is negative}, the minimum being $+0.107$. That is ordinal and uses
every run (robustness history in App.~\ref{app:robust}).

\textbf{The curve is not a step, and both ends can be inflated.} It overshoots at step $1000$;
taking the peak as the post value, or the lowest checkpoint alone as the pre value, inflates the
effect, so both ends use the pre-registered sets. The overshoot survives correction in one cell
of four (accounting in App.~\ref{app:robust}), so step $1000$ is no distinct phase.

\textbf{It is not specific to the model we picked.} Repeating the grid on four Pythia sizes
(70m/160m/410m/1b $\times$ 6 checkpoints $\times$ 8 seeds, 192 runs) reproduces the transition in
\textbf{all four} (per-size $p_{\mathrm{BH}}$ in App.~\ref{app:robust}). Its
\emph{timing} moves later with size and then saturates: 70m is already super-critical at the
earliest checkpoint probed, 160m crosses between steps $128$ and $256$, and 410m and 1b both
cross between $256$ and $512$. Learning rate is confounded with size across the suite (rates in App.~\ref{app:robust}) and the
two sizes sharing a rate are the two sharing a bracket, so we report the shift as an ordering,
not a size effect. An orthogonal instrument agrees on the onset:
\citet{ar_tempcrit} place the emergence of critical structure in Pythia-160m at $k_c{\approx}10^2$
steps by power spectra and part-of-speech correlation --- no damage spreading --- adjacent to our
$128$--$256$ bracket. Both grids are coarse there, so this is agreement to within about a factor
of two, not a reproduction. The plateau \emph{level} does not order with size
(levels in App.~\ref{app:robust}); at four sizes the smallest attainable permutation $p$ is
$1/4!\,{=}\,0.042$, so no capacity axis could have been established, and none is visible. We
report that null as the closest thing here to a previously retracted capacity claim.

\textbf{It is not a restatement of the loss curve.} The transition sits where everything else in
training changes, so \lca{} might be an expensive perplexity proxy. It is not: held-out loss falls
\emph{monotonically} at all four sizes while \lca{} overshoots at three, and a non-monotone
function of a monotone variable is not a monotone transform of it. They also disagree about
\emph{where} (details in App.~\ref{app:robust}). We use the curve's \emph{shape} and not
its level, the evaluation text not being Pythia's training data.

\textbf{Which quantity carries the claim.} A third lattice size settles it. Over $N{=}48/96/192$
the $\lca$ plateau is \textbf{intensive across a $4\times$ range} --- slope $N^{-0.04}$,
varying by $5\%$ of its mean --- while \dn{} falls as $N^{-1.02}$, i.e.\ \textbf{$1/N$}
(levels in App.~\ref{app:robust}).
That is what the construction predicts: $\lca$ is a cone-growth \emph{rate} fitted before
saturation; \dn{} is a density ratio whose numerator stays in a cone while its denominator is
delocalised. So $\lca$ carries the claim and \dn{}
corroborates at a stated lattice size, never as a lattice-free property. Seed-to-seed variance
also collapses ${\sim}3\times$ across the transition (Levene $p\le1.7\times10^{-4}$ at both
sizes), an observation rather than a pre-registered test. \lca{} is continuous and unthresholded, so
this sharpening is not an artefact of a discontinuous metric \citep{mirage}.

\section{What the damping length measures: free generation absorbs nothing}\label{sec:realgen}
We tested the instrument's central assumption --- that a ring number says something about the
model --- on \emph{real autoregressive generation}: two CRN twins from a prompt sharing one
uniform stream, one forced to a different token at one position, both continuing freely. The null
arm diverges by exactly zero, as asserted.

The result is unambiguous and negative. On \texttt{pythia-70m}, \texttt{-160m} and \texttt{-410m}
(32 trials each) the injected error \textbf{persists in every single trial}
($P_{\text{persist}}{=}1.000$). Token identity is harsh once an injection shifts alignment, so we
also measured distributionally: twin next-token TV, normalised by that between \emph{independent}
continuations, settles at $\mathrm{TV_{norm}}\approx0.97$.

The mechanism is structural. \textbf{Free generation never resamples a token}: once emitted an
error stays in context permanently, whereas the ring CA revisits every site, and in-place
resampling is what makes healing possible. The damping length, \dn{} and the light cone therefore
characterise \emph{the iterated-resampling construction} and not decoding --- which also explains
a light-cone velocity depending on $r$ but not the model, and the failure in
\S\ref{sec:crosslevel}. It does \emph{not} touch \S\ref{sec:dev}: the construction is
held \emph{fixed} across checkpoints --- same ring, radius, temperature, coupling, seeds --- so a change
measured across checkpoints cannot come from the apparatus. The licence is therefore
\emph{comparative}, checkpoint against checkpoint, and never absolute: we do not claim any model
\emph{is} critical, since that would need the ring, the radius and \dz{} to be principled rather
than merely fixed, and they are choices.

Damage spreading is a property of \emph{(model, coupling)}, not of the model alone
\citep{kohring1992,grassberger1995}; our coupling choice, its justification and its measured
cost are stated precisely in App.~\ref{app:robust}, and relative comparisons are unaffected
because the coupling is a common mode.

\section{The instrument's boundary: token- vs.\ activation-space}\label{sec:crosslevel}
A black-box instrument earns its keep if its readings track what internals would otherwise be
needed to see. Across two model families and two within-model axes they do not: the pairing is
family-dependent (Pythia $r{=}{+}0.71$, GPT-2 $r{=}{-}0.43$; the pooled $p{=}0.025$ is a
Simpson's-paradox artifact), the temperature axis is confounded, and the radius axis is null
because $\lca(r)$ is model-invariant. The reason is structural: the white-box exponent is
architectural and \emph{tangent}, while $\lca$ is \emph{finite}. A clean \textbf{negative} --- the
two levels are distinct and non-proxying.

\section{Related work}
Iterated-LLM views exist qualitatively \citep{logos_ca,telephone,paraphrase2cycle} and rigorously
for masked-LM Glauber dynamics \citep{glauber_mlm}; our novelty is the measurement layer, not the
framing. Edge-of-chaos measurement is canonical \citep{langton1990computation,bertschinger2004real} and
already applied to transformers \citep{lyap_selfattn} and LLMs \citep{edgeofchaos2024,qle}; we
claim neither the concept nor the criticality$\leftrightarrow$capability link, and
absorbing-state scaling laws in deep networks are established \citep{tamai_dp} --- we report no
universality class. Temperature-driven order--disorder transitions have several prior
treatments \citep{critical_temp,critical_temp2,critical_temp3,ar_tempcrit,phase_detect}; we claim
a calibrated route, not the quantity. Perturbation propagation in \emph{activation} space
\citep{sparc,tft,residual_dynamics} and over compound-AI graphs \citep{quiver} is adjacent but in
a different space; we avoid ``repair'' \citep{hydra,selfrepair} and ``self-correction''
\citep{sparc}, categorically different from a spatial damping length. \citet{sampler_oracle} is
the calibration kin; the census descends from \citet{hanson1997computational}.

\section{Limitations}
Neither construction samples the model's actual distribution --- masked conditionals are
globally inconsistent, the autoregressive rule is resampled in place on a ring --- so all claims
are properties of the dynamics. Rings are small ($N\le384$); the
developmental result rests on one model \emph{family} (four sizes and three lattice sizes within
it, so a second family is the next requirement) and on a narrow temperature window, outside which
the probe saturates either way (\S\ref{sec:dev}). \dn{}'s absolute scale is doubly qualified ---
it moves with $N$, and its numerator's coupling is not extremal --- so only its relative behaviour
is quoted, and census ground truth is synthetic only. \textbf{Statistical scope.} The developmental family is
pre-registered with BH-FDR at the run level and the ECA test uses the rule as its unit; $\lca$
statistics exclude runs whose damage never ignited, for which it is undefined, with $n$ stated.
The ladder rests on no hypothesis test and the cross-level negative on sign-consistency rather
than a surviving $p$-value. A previously reported capacity$\to$sensitivity axis was
pseudoreplicated and is retracted.

\section{Responsible use and conclusion}
This instrument measures a construction built \emph{from} a language model, from sampled outputs
alone. That is its affordance: it characterises models behind an API, where weights are
unavailable and activation-space methods cannot reach. It creates no new generative capability. The misuse we consider most likely
is a misreading of our own numbers, which is why \S\ref{sec:realgen} is in the body: a damping
length measured here describes an in-place-resampling ring, and real generation absorbs
\emph{no} injected token error. These quantities must \textbf{not} be cited as
evidence about the robustness, error tolerance or self-correction of a deployed decoder --- a
$\lca$ below zero is not a safety property. That boundary generalises: a metric should be made to
reproduce known
answers before it is believed about unknown ones. We offer the ladder as a method, not only as our step
one --- it demoted our own ECA ordering and caught a $\lca$ reported where it is undefined.

\textbf{Temperature window} (\S\ref{sec:dev}): the floor at $T{=}0.3$ --- ignition
$0.20\to0.21$ across training, \lca{} hugging zero --- and the ceiling at $T{=}0.9$ and $1.1$,
where pre-training \lca{} is already ${+}0.19$ and ${+}0.30$ with ignition $0.65$ and $0.98$.

\textbf{Coupling, stated precisely} (\S\ref{sec:instrument}, \S\ref{sec:realgen}): the
diversity floor \dz{} necessarily uses a different coupling from the damage numerator --- a floor
sharing the numerator's draws is identically zero, since identical twins stay identical --- and
the cost is bounded: sweeping the shared-draw fraction from $0$ to $0.9$ moves \dn{} by $4\%$.
Our inverse-CDF sampling is the
\emph{monotone} coupling. On DK's binary alphabet it coincides with the maximal-correlation
member of the admissible family \citep{hinrichsen1997}, which is why the DK identity is exact;
on a vocabulary it does not, so the LM damage numbers sit inside the family rather than at an
extremum --- excess disagreement $1.3$--$5.4\%$ across the temperature sweep. The choice is
justified by replica-independence --- each replica's next state depends on its own state and the
shared noise alone --- which a pairwise maximal coupling lacks. One fact explains both ends of
this paper: damage-spreading theory was developed on \emph{binary} alphabets, which is why the
DK rung is exact and why its coupling guarantee does not survive $|V|\approx5\times10^{4}$. The
strongest calibration and the sharpest limitation share one cause, so an imported guarantee must
be checked against the alphabet.

\textbf{Demoted ECA ordering} (\S\ref{sec:validation}): the group-mean $\lambda$ ordering we
first computed was never a measurement --- five of seven ordered rules return exactly
$-0.4\ln10$, the constant a log-linear fit yields when damage dies immediately.

\textbf{Estimator history} (\S\ref{sec:dev}): the ordinal headline survived a later change to
unignited-run handling that moved every mean, which is why it, and not any effect size, is the
claim.
